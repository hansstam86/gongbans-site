1 · The one thing to understand

What is a gongban?

A gongban (公板, "public board") is a finished, working circuit-board design that a Chinese chip house or design house publishes so that many companies can build products on it. Most consumer electronics from China start from one: a brand picks the board, then adds its own casing, software, logo and packaging. For you it means you don't design the electronics from scratch — you pick a proven board, and your work is everything around it.

3 · Learn these cheaply

Common first-timer mistakes

  • Ordering tooling before anyone has paid. A mould is the most expensive way to find out nobody wants your product. Deposits or signed pilots first, steel later.
  • Treating the board as the product. The board is the easy part. Enclosure, power, firmware, packaging and certificates are each their own project, and each one will slip.
  • Asking for a price with no BOM, no quantity and no spec. You get a number made up to keep you talking. It won't survive the real quote, and by then you have planned around it.
  • Believing the CE logo printed on the sample. A printed mark proves nothing. You sign the declaration of conformity, you own the test reports. Budget for the testing.
  • Skipping the licence on community boards. Some forbid commercial use or oblige you to publish your changes. Read the licence on the board's page before you build a business on it.
4 · The words

Glossary

Gongban (公板)
A published, proven circuit-board design that many products are built on. The thing this site catalogues.
Reference design
The English term for roughly the same thing: a working design a chip maker publishes to show how its chip should be used. Gongbans are reference designs that turned into products.
ODM / OEM
ODM: they design and build, you put your brand on it. OEM: they build your design. Shenzhen uses both words loosely; ask what is meant.
IDH / design house / solution house
The company that designs the gongban and sells the "solution" (board plus firmware) to brands. Many of the publishers under Who makes them are design houses.
BOM
Bill of materials: every part on the board with its part number and quantity. A factory quotes from it; no BOM, no real price.
Gerber
The file format the PCB factory uses to make the bare board (copper layers, drill holes). If a board comes with free Gerbers, you can have it made as it is.
EVT / DVT / PVT
Engineering, design and production validation: the three build rounds between prototype and mass production. Each round catches a different kind of mistake. Playbook, section 3 →
CE / RED
CE is the EU conformity mark you declare yourself and are liable for. RED is the Radio Equipment Directive, which applies as soon as your product has Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or any other radio. Testing costs money; plan for it. Playbook, section 6 →
MOQ
Minimum order quantity: the smallest run a factory or a parts supplier will accept.
Open files
Design files (schematic, PCB layout, Gerber, BOM) published for anyone to download. In the catalogue, Free download means exactly that; Ask the distributor means you have to request them; Community project means check the licence first.

More terms (Incoterms, HS codes, EORI, UN 38.3…) are in the playbook glossary.
