# Machine Access Box — Founder Start Pack

> An RFID or fingerprint interlock for workshop machines that checks the user's training level, logs usage and enables power, with no subscription.

Concept from the GONGBANS inventor. Not validated. All numbers are estimates — check them. Check every board's licence before commercial use.

## 1. Summary

- **Problem:** Open workshops need to know that the person starting the laser cutter has been trained on it, and to log who used what; today that is a sticker, a key cabinet or a per-machine software subscription.
- **Customer:** Makerspaces and open workshops, school and university workshops, repair cafés and tool libraries. The space's board, the workshop manager or the university's lab budget pays; the volunteer instructor sets training levels.
- **Product:** A box per machine with a card or fingerprint reader, a relay that enables the machine's control circuit, an LED ring and buzzer, a local members-and-training server (FabAccess-compatible) with usage logs and maintenance counters; no per-machine fee. Target price €149–199 (estimate).
- **Why now:** FabAccess is being rolled out across ten workshops with public funding, which proves the sector wants machine access control; the commercial option bills per machine, and the catalogue has readers, relays and cores ready.
- **The ask:** ~€40–55k to certification and a first 1,000 boxes (estimate); network pre-orders cover a third.

## 2. Validate demand first

<!-- validate-demand:start -->
**Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5).** The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

### The gate: this idea's kill question

> Do 10 makerspaces from one network pre-order 3 boxes each at €169? Pass = 10 orders of 3 in 8 weeks.

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: written answers from at least ten decision-makers who hold the budget, at least three of whom put something on paper: a signed letter of intent, a paid pilot, or a dated purchase order for the first units. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

### Who you are looking for

**The audience named in the idea:** Makerspaces and open workshops (Verbund Offener Werkstätten, FabLabs), school and university workshops, repair cafés; the space's board or the workshop manager pays.

**The problem as the pack states it:** Open workshops need to know that the person starting the laser cutter has been trained on it, and to log who used what; today that is a sticker, a key cabinet or a per-machine software subscription.

Talk to the people who have the problem *and* the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

### Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

- **Verbund Offener Werkstätten (DE)** · https://www.offene-werkstaetten.org/ — German network of open workshops and makerspaces.
- **hackerspaces.org wiki** · https://wiki.hackerspaces.org/ — directory of makerspaces worldwide; e-mail the ones near you.
- **Hackaday / hackaday.io** · https://hackaday.io/ — maker audience; visibility more than buyers.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

- **Search for communities around the problem, not the product:** forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
- **Trade associations and their regional meetings** for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
- **LinkedIn:** search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
- **Your deposit list from the kill question** and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

### The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

1. **15–20 real conversations** (ten budget-holders and ten users), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
2. **A fake door.** One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€149–199, the pack's estimate), and one button. For a business buyer the button is "request a pilot" that leads to a short form and a letter of intent.
3. **Send it** to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
4. **Count** against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

### Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

```email
Thanks for the time. I am looking into how makerspaces and open workshops, school and university workshops, repair cafés deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. How do you control who uses the dangerous machines today, and what happened the last time someone untrained used one?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] An RFID or fingerprint interlock for workshop machines that checks the user's training level, logs usage and enables power, with no subscription. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €149–199, and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.
```

### Pre-order / pilot message (forum post or direct message)

```email
Subject: Machine Access Box: looking for 3 pilot sites before we build it

I am [your name], [your background]. We are considering building a Machine Access Box: An RFID or fingerprint interlock for workshop machines that checks the user's training level, logs usage and enables power, with no subscription. Before we spend money on tooling I want to find out whether this solves a real problem for makerspaces and open workshops, school and university workshops, repair cafés.

If it would, I am looking for three pilot customers willing to sign a short letter of intent now and pay €149–199 per unit for the first batch, delivered in [month]. The LOI is non-binding on the price; it tells me the problem is real.

Two questions, no pitch: how do you handle this today, and what would make you say no to the above? Twenty minutes on a call this week, or a reply here, both help.
```

### Reading the signal

| Real signal | False positive |
|---|---|
| A refundable deposit, a signed LOI, a paid pilot, a calendar invite for the prototype | "Sounds cool", "I would totally buy that", likes and upvotes |
| They describe the problem unprompted and in detail, with numbers | They agree with your description of the problem |
| They have already paid for a workaround (a product, a service, their own time) | They have never tried anything and are not bothered |
| Landing-page conversion to deposit or form above the pack's pass mark, from cold traffic | Conversion from friends, family and your own network |
| A named budget and a named decision-maker (B2B) | An enthusiastic user who cannot buy |

### Go / no-go

- **Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit)** when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
- **Change the idea** when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
- **Stop** when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

## 3. Business plan

### Problem & customer

Machine access control is the recurring project of every makerspace: FabAccess (offene-werkstaetten.org, a FOSDEM talk, a publicly funded ten-workshop roll-out in 2023–24) shows the sector organising around it; commercial Fabman bills per bridge (pricing unverified; get a quote before claiming a cost advantage). What spaces lack is the box: a finished, certified, pretty unit per machine that a volunteer can install in an hour and that talks to the open server.

**Who pays, B2B:** the space (membership fees, grants) or the institution; a space buys 5–30 boxes at once. **Who uses:** every member, so the reader must work with the cards they already have (Mifare, DESFire, student cards).

### Market size (estimates — check)

- **TAM:** ~2,000 makerspaces and FabLabs in Europe plus school and university workshops; 10–30 machines each. Theoretical €10–20M.
- **SAM:** spaces with paid membership or institutional backing: estimate 1,200 × 12 machines → €2.4M.
- **SOM (3 yrs):** 6,000–10,000 boxes; year-1: 1,000–1,500 through two networks and a university.

### Competition (3 searches, incl. the November 2026 external market check)

| Alternative | What it is / price found | Why we still win |
|---|---|---|
| Fabman | Per-machine bridge with a subscription (pricing unverified) | No per-machine fee; open server; get the quote before claiming |
| FabAccess (open source, funded roll-out) | Open access-control software with DIY hardware | We are the finished box that runs it; compatibility, not competition |
| MakeACS, MakerCard and similar | Smaller access-control projects and products | Finished hardware and support |
| Key cabinets and stickers | Free | No log, no training check, no maintenance counter |

**Wedge:** the finished box for the open server the sector is already adopting: certified, installer-simple, no per-machine fee.

### Product & value proposition

- Reader: 13.56 MHz (Mifare/DESFire/student cards) or fingerprint; LED ring (red/green/amber), buzzer; relay output for the machine's control circuit (24 V / low-voltage enable), never the motor current.
- Modes: enable while card present, enable for a session, two-person rule for dangerous machines, instructor override.
- Server: local (Raspberry Pi class or the space's server), FabAccess-compatible API, members and training levels, usage log, maintenance counters, CSV; no cloud of ours.
- Install: DIN or wall box, 12–24 V in, two terminals out, one-hour fit by a volunteer with the machine's manual.

### Unit economics (estimates, EUR per unit)

| Item | 1k units | 5k units |
|---|---|---|
| Electronics BOM (estimate, mid of €30–45) | 37.5 | 30.0 |
| PCBA assembly + test | 4.0 | 2.6 |
| Enclosure / mechanics (tooling amortised separately) | 7.0 | 4.9 |
| Packaging, cable, leaflet | 2.5 | 1.9 |
| Freight + duty to EU (air at 1k, sea at 5k; check the HS code) | 3.0 | 1.5 |
| Certification amortised (€9k, estimate) | 9.0 | 1.8 |
| **Landed cost** | **63.0** | **42.7** |
| Channel cost (direct to spaces and networks, ~€15) | 15.0 | 15.0 |
| Net price received (€174 incl. 19% VAT → €146.2 ex VAT) | 146.2 | 146.2 |
| **Gross margin per unit** | **€68.2 (47%)** | **€88.5 (61%)** |

Lesson: a space buys a dozen at a time; discount the dozen, not the single box. Fingerprint readers raise the BOM; keep them optional.

### Go-to-market

**First 100 customers:**

1. Ten spaces from one network (Verbund Offener Werkstätten, a FabLab network) pre-ordering three boxes each.
2. One university workshop as the institutional reference.
3. Repair cafés and tool libraries for the cheapest variant.

**Scale channels:** the networks' meetings and mailing lists, FabAccess's community, university procurement frameworks.

### 12-month plan

| Quarter | Milestones |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Kill-question pre-orders; works-like box on Core2 + RFID2 + Relay with FabAccess; three spaces test. |
| Q2 | Custom PCB; enclosure; EVT 30 in three spaces; fingerprint variant. |
| Q3 | DVT; certification; 500 boxes; network pre-orders shipped. |
| Q4 | University framework; 1,500 boxes; maintenance-counter features. |

**Team:** one embedded engineer, one backend developer close to the FabAccess community, one founder from a makerspace.

**Funding to first production (estimates):** NRE €14k + certification €8k + first 1,000 boxes landed ~€35k ≈ €57k; minus pre-orders ~€15k ≈ €42k.

### Top risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Spaces expect a safety interlock | State clearly: control-circuit enable, not a safety function; e-stop stays; installation guide |
| Fabman turns out cheaper than assumed | Get the quote; compete on openness and no fee |
| Card diversity | Reader supports the common standards; UID-based enrolment; fingerprint option |
| Volunteer installations go wrong | Two terminals, clear manual, photos per machine type |

### Certification checklist (EU)

- **RED** via the module's own radio reports (EN 300 328 / EN 300 220 as applicable, EN 301 489 EMC), **EN 18031** cybersecurity for the connected variant, **EN 62368-1** safety (USB-powered, no mains inside), **RoHS/REACH**, **WEEE** registration per country, **CRA** vulnerability-handling process.
- Test with the DVT build in the real enclosure; budget a re-spin for EMC.
- Lab cost: a few thousand euros for a USB-powered device with a pre-certified module, more with battery or multiple radios (estimate; get two quotes).

### Validation toolkit

- **Kill question:** Do 10 makerspaces from one network pre-order 3 boxes each at €169? Pass = 10 orders of 3 in 8 weeks.
- **Cheapest experiment:** Three prototype boxes on three machines of one makerspace for a month; ask the board whether they would buy twelve at €169.
- **5-question interview:** 1) How do you control who uses the laser cutter today? 2) Have you looked at Fabman or built your own; what happened? 3) Who pays for equipment and how much per year? 4) What cards do your members carry? 5) At €169 per machine with no fee, how many machines?
- **Landing page:** headline "Only trained hands start the machine." Bullets: your cards, your server, usage log, €169 per machine, no fee. Button: pre-order 3 (refundable deposit).
- **Pass threshold:** 10 orders of 3 within 8 weeks.

### First production checklist

- 10 golden samples measured and photographed; one per enclosure colour.
- DFM review with the PCBA house: test pads, programming jig, serial/MAC provisioning, who holds the signing keys (you).
- MOQ talk: module, enclosure, packaging; get the next price break in writing.
- QC: AQL II 2.5/4.0; 100% functional test incl. radio; burn-in sample.
- Packaging and label: CE, WEEE bin, responsible-person address, model/serial; manual with safety and disposal text.
- Incoterm and shipping: DDP by courier for the first run; forwarder quote for the second.
