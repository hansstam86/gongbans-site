1 · Summary · the one-page version

Summary

  • Problem: Getting up at night in the dark is a classic trip hazard for older people. PIR night lights trigger late (once you're already walking and in their field of view), ceiling lights are too bright, and people simply don't switch anything on.
  • Customer: Adult children buying for a parent who lives alone (they pay), the older person (uses), care-housing operators fitting out flats (pay at scale). Secondary: parents of toddlers, anyone who shares a bedroom.
  • Product: A plug-in base unit with a 24 GHz presence radar aimed at the bed edge, driving a 2 m warm-white (2200 K) skirting strip at 5–10% brightness that ramps up softly. Optional second unit for the doorway/hallway. Optional, opt-in weekly "night activity" summary to family. Not a medical device, no fall claims.
  • Why now: Ageing-in-place policy across the EU, waiting lists for care places, and €3–4 low-power radar modules that detect someone sitting still on a bed edge — which PIR cannot.
  • The ask: ~€90k to certification and 1,000 sets; fund it with care-housing pilot orders and a pharmacy/home-care buyer's trial order. Kill question first: will 2 of 5 home-care buyers place a trial order at €69?
2 · Validate demand · before anything else

Validate demand first

Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5). The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

The gate: this idea's kill question

Will home-care shops/pharmacy chains list it at €69? Ask 5 buyers with a working sample; need 2 trial orders.

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: a fake-door landing page that older people living independently, their families, care-housing fit-outs actually visited, with a measured conversion to a waitlist or refundable deposit, plus the interview notes behind it. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

Who you are looking for

The audience named in the idea: Older people living independently, their families, care-housing fit-outs; also parents of toddlers.

The problem as the pack states it: Getting up at night in the dark is a classic trip hazard for older people. PIR night lights trigger late (once you're already walking and in their field of view), ceiling lights are too bright, and people simply don't switch anything on.

Talk to the people who have the problem and the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

  • r/AgingParents · reddit.com/r/AgingParents/ — adult children caring for parents: the buyer for most elderly-care devices.
  • r/CaregiverSupport · reddit.com/r/CaregiverSupport/ — family carers.
  • Carers UK forum · carersuk.org/forum/ — family carers in the UK.
  • AGE Platform Europe · age-platform.eu/ — EU network of organisations of and for older people.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

  • Search for communities around the problem, not the product: forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
  • Trade associations and their regional meetings for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
  • LinkedIn: search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
  • Your deposit list from the kill question and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

  1. 15–20 real conversations (twenty people from the audience line), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
  2. A fake door. One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€59–89 (estimate), the pack's estimate), and one button. For a consumer the button takes a refundable deposit or a waitlist e-mail; a deposit is worth ten e-mails.
  3. Send it to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
  4. Count against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

Thanks for the time. I am looking into how older people living independently, their families, care-housing fit-outs deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. When your parent gets up at night to go to the bathroom, how do they find their way, and has anything happened on that route in the dark?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] Plug-in floor-level light strips that softly light the way from bed to bathroom the moment someone sits up at night, and go dark once they're back — radar sees people, not pets on the floor. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €59–89 (estimate), and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.

Pre-order / pilot message (forum post or direct message)

Subject: Night Path Light: would you pay €59–89 (estimate) for this? Looking for 20 people to tell me I am wrong

I am [your name], [your background]. I am considering building a Night Path Light: Plug-in floor-level light strips that softly light the way from bed to bathroom the moment someone sits up at night, and go dark once they're back — radar sees people, not pets on the floor. Before I order a single part I want to know whether this is a real problem for people like you or only in my head.

If you would want one at €59–89 (estimate), the page at [link] takes a fully refundable €[10–20] deposit (or a waitlist e-mail if you prefer); if twenty people do that, I build it, if not, everyone gets their money back and I have learned something.

Either way, two questions: how do you deal with this today, and what would make you say no? Honest answers are worth more to me than deposits.

Go / no-go

  • Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit) when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
  • Change the idea when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
  • Stop when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

3 · Business plan · estimates, not a forecast

Business plan

Problem & customer

Most falls at home happen on routine trips, and the bed-to-bathroom trip at night is the one families worry about. The fix is boring: light, early, not blinding. PIR night lights see you once you're walking towards them; they don't see the 20 seconds you sit on the bed edge finding your slippers. A presence radar aimed across the bed edge does.

  • Who pays, B2C: the 45–65-year-old child organising a parent's home. They buy in pharmacies, medical-supply shops (Sanitätshaus in DE/AT, thuiszorgwinkel in NL), and online after a scare.
  • Who pays, B2B: care-housing and assisted-living operators (Betreutes Wohnen, serviceflats), housing associations' senior programmes, home-adaptation contractors paid by long-term care funds (in DE the Pflegekasse pays for home adaptations — check whether lighting qualifies; don't promise it).
  • Who uses: the older person. Zero buttons in daily use. It must not glare, must not click, must not need a phone.

Market size (estimates — check)

  • TAM: EU-27 people aged 75+ ≈ 45M (Eurostat order of magnitude — check); about one third live alone → ~15M households. One set at €69 → ~€1.0B theoretical.
  • SAM: DE/AT/NL/BE/Nordics, households where family or a care provider actively adapts the home: assume 15% of ~5M lone 75+ households → 750k; at €65 average → ~€49M.
  • SOM (3 years): 1.5% of SAM via pharmacies/home-care shops and 3–5 care-housing operators → ~11k sets, ~€0.6M. Year 1: 1,000–2,000.

Product & value proposition

  • Base unit: plug-in housing at the socket next to the bed; radar module in a small tethered "eye" you stick to the bedside table or bed frame at mattress height, aimed at the bed-edge zone (distance gates 0.7–1.5 m). Drives a 2 m 2200 K COB strip that you stick along the skirting towards the door.
  • Behaviour: sit-up detected → strip ramps 0→8% over 2 s; stays on while presence is detected anywhere in the path; fades out 90 s after the person is back in bed and still. Dusk-only (light sensor). One slider: brightness. One switch: off/auto.
  • Doorway unit (optional): same radar + strip for the hallway/bathroom door; syncs over the air with the base (ESP-NOW or BLE).
  • Family summary (opt-in, Wi-Fi SKU): "nights with activity / average number of trips this week" by e-mail. Treat it as health-adjacent data (see risks).
  • Honest limit: radar sees bodies, not species. A cat or dog moving on the bed will turn the light on too. That's harmless (soft light), but don't claim "ignores pets". Distance gates reduce floor-level pet triggers in the doorway unit; test it.

Pricing (estimates): starter set (base + eye + 2 m strip) €69 retail; doorway unit €45; Wi-Fi summary SKU €89. Care-housing operators (100+ flats): €45 per set ex VAT, installed by their caretaker.

Go-to-market

First 100 customers:

  • Two care-housing operators or senior-housing programmes of a housing association: 25-flat paid pilots each, with a short resident questionnaire after 6 weeks.
  • Adult-children communities: caregiver forums (e.g. pflege.de forum, Alzheimer society newsletters, Facebook groups for caring relatives), and local senior-advice centres (Pflegestützpunkte in DE, Wohnberatung in AT) — give them a demo unit.
  • Five independent medical-supply shops in one city: consignment of 10 sets each with a demo board.

Then scale: pharmacy chains' wholesale/assortment buyers, medical-supply shop buying groups, online (Amazon.de, bol.com), home-adaptation contractors, occupational therapists as recommenders. Later: white-label for an assistive-tech brand.

12-month plan

  • Q1: the kill question with 5 buyers using a hand-built sample; 20 family interviews; 10 home prototypes on LD2410S + ESP32-C3 dev boards.
  • Q2: rev A, EN 60598 pre-assessment, care-housing pilots (50 sets), measure false triggers and "light came too late" complaints.
  • Q3: rev B, certification, tooling, 1,000-set run; retail trial orders; D2C launch.
  • Q4: doorway unit and Wi-Fi summary SKU; second operator; NL/BE launch.

Team: founder (sales into care/retail), embedded engineer (part-time, ESP32 + radar tuning), lighting/power engineer (contract — the mains + luminaire safety part), industrial designer (contract, 6 weeks — this product lives on looks and large-print usability), Shenzhen partner for PCBA, strips and housings.

Certification checklist (EU)

  • CE — RED (24 GHz radar + 2.4 GHz radio): EN 300 440 (24 GHz SRD) and EN 300 328 (Wi-Fi/BLE), EN 301 489-1/-3/-17, EN 62311/EN 62479 (RF exposure). The radar module's own test reports help but don't replace yours.
  • LVD / safety: the product is a luminaire — EN 60598-1 + EN 60598-2-12 (mains socket-outlet mounted night lights); EN 62471 photobiological safety; EN 55015 + EN 61547 (lighting EMC). Ecodesign/energy-labelling rules for light sources apply to the strip (check whether it's a "containing product").
  • EN 18031-1: for the Wi-Fi summary SKU (internet-connected). The base SKU with only ESP-NOW/BLE between units — get the lab's view on scope.
  • CRA: product with digital elements; SBOM, update path and vulnerability handling by Dec 2027.
  • RoHS, REACH, WEEE, packaging registration.
  • GDPR: summary SKU only; opt-in, aggregates, DPIA, EU hosting.
  • MDR boundary (state it clearly): this is a lighting product. The moment you claim it prevents or detects falls, monitors a medical condition, or alerts carers to a health event, it may become a medical device under MDR 2017/745 (class I or higher) — a different budget and timeline. Don't.
  • Toy directive: no (even in the toddler use case, it's a luminaire, not a toy).
  • Estimated test costs: €15–22k (base + doorway), +€3–5k for the Wi-Fi SKU.

Validation toolkit

  • Kill question: Will home-care shops / pharmacy chains list it at €69? Show 5 buyers a working sample; you need 2 trial orders. Cheapest experiment: a 3D-printed working sample, a 60-second video of the sit-up trigger in a dark room, and five buyer meetings booked via LinkedIn and the trade fair calendar (REHACARE in Düsseldorf is the obvious venue — check dates).
  • Interview script (5) — for the adult child: 1. What worries you most about your parent at night? 2. What have you already tried (night lights, leaving the light on, sensors)? What happened to it? 3. Where would you buy this — pharmacy, medical-supply shop, online? Who else would decide? 4. Would your parent accept something plugged in next to the bed? What would make them unplug it? 5. €69 for the bed-to-bathroom set: buy, think about it, or no? What would make it a yes?
  • Landing page: Headline: "Light the way before the first step." Bullets: "Turns on when they sit up — not when they're already walking in the dark." / "Soft, warm, floor-level light. No glare, no switches, no app." / "Plug in, stick the strip, done in 5 minutes."
  • Pre-order pass: 2 buyer trial orders (≥50 units total) or ≥150 D2C pre-orders at €59 early-bird within 30 days.

First production checklist

  • Samples: 5 PCBA samples from two assemblers; strip samples from 3 suppliers with CCT/CRI and flicker measurements; golden sample incl. measured luminance at 5% and ramp curve.
  • DFM review: plug-in housing (pin retention, creepage, ultrasonic weld), eye cable strain relief, strip adhesive on painted skirting (test 4 paint types).
  • MOQ talk: radar modules (ask Hi-Link/distributor for 1k pricing and lead time), strips 2k m, housings 1–3k shots.
  • QC/AQL: ISO 2859-1 level II, AQL 0.65/2.5; 100% hipot test on the mains unit, functional test (radar detect at 1 m target, PWM at 5% and 100%), 4 h burn-in.
  • Packaging: large-print quick guide with photos, multilingual (DE/NL/FR/EN), retail hang-tab box.
  • Incoterms: FOB Shenzhen; later DDP for retail customers that want a EU-delivered price.
