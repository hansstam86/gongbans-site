# Night-Time Door Prompt — Founder Start Pack

> When someone heads for the front door at night, a familiar recorded voice gently says "it's 3 a.m., time to go back to bed", the hallway light comes on, and the carer gets a quiet notification.

Concept from the GONGBANS inventor. Not validated. All numbers are estimates — check them. Check every board's licence before commercial use.

## 1. Summary

- **Problem:** Families caring at home for someone with early-stage memory problems lose sleep over night-time wandering. Door alarms blare, wake the whole house and frighten the person. Door contacts only react once the door is already open.
- **Customer:** Family carers (adult children, partners) who pay out of pocket; small-scale living facilities and home-care providers as a second channel. The person living with memory problems is the one who hears the prompt.
- **Product:** A small hallway unit with a 24 GHz trajectory radar that tells "walking towards the door" from "walking past it". Inside a night window it plays a personal recorded message, switches on a hallway light, and only notifies the carer if the prompt is ignored. Wi-Fi via a Tuya module for fast phone notifications; local-only mode without phone alerts.
- **Why now:** Europe's ageing population means more people living at home with memory problems and more family carers. Trajectory radar modules give direction of movement without a camera for a few euros.
- **The ask:** ~€95k to certification and 1,000 units, funded by a 10-family pilot, then pre-orders via carer communities.

This is a home safety and comfort product: an alert and a voice prompt. It is **not a medical device**, does not diagnose, monitor or treat any condition, and is **not a restraint**. See the MDR boundary in the certification section.

## 2. Validate demand first

**Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5).** The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

### The gate: this idea's kill question

> Do 10 carer families still use it after 4 weeks, and does the prompt actually turn the person around at least half the time? Also: will carers accept a Tuya-cloud dependency for alerts?

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: a measured conversion on a fake-door landing page for the consumer side and, for the business side, at least three decision-makers who put something on paper: a letter of intent, a paid pilot or a purchase order. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

- **Search for communities around the problem, not the product:** forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.

- **Your deposit list from the kill question** and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

### The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

1. **15–20 real conversations** (ten buyers from the business side and ten from the consumer side), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
2. **A fake door.** One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€89–119 (estimate), the pack's estimate), and one button. Two buttons: a refundable deposit or waitlist for consumers, "request a pilot" for businesses.
3. **Send it** to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
4. **Count** against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

### Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

```email
Thanks for the time. I am looking into how families caring at home for someone with early-stage memory problems deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. Tell me about the nights: has your relative tried to go out at night, how did you find out, and what do you do now so you can sleep?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] When someone heads for the front door at night, a familiar recorded voice gently says 'it's 3 a.m., time to go back to bed' and the hallway light comes on — and the carer gets a quiet notification. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €89–119 (estimate), and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.
```

### Reading the signal

| Real signal | False positive |
|---|---|
| A refundable deposit, a signed LOI, a paid pilot, a calendar invite for the prototype | "Sounds cool", "I would totally buy that", likes and upvotes |
| They describe the problem unprompted and in detail, with numbers | They agree with your description of the problem |
| They have already paid for a workaround (a product, a service, their own time) | They have never tried anything and are not bothered |
| Landing-page conversion to deposit or form above the pack's pass mark, from cold traffic | Conversion from friends, family and your own network |
| A named budget and a named decision-maker (B2B) | An enthusiastic user who cannot buy |

### Go / no-go

- **Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit)** when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
- **Change the idea** when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
- **Stop** when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

## 3. Business plan

### Problem & customer

At 3 a.m. the person gets up, is disoriented and heads for the front door. The carer sleeps lightly for months. Alarms create panic; locking doors raises safety and ethical issues; cameras feel like surveillance in the person's own home.

- **Who pays:** the family carer (often an adult child living nearby or the partner). Typical budget: €50–150 for a device that "lets me sleep". Some care insurers and municipalities subsidise assistive technology — rules vary per country (in Germany the "Pflegehilfsmittel"/housing-adaptation routes exist; check eligibility — don't promise it).
- **Who uses:** the person living with memory problems (passively — hears the voice); the carer (sets up, gets notifications).
- **Ethics:** the voice is a prompt, not a barrier. Design and marketing must respect dignity and autonomy; include an ethical-use guide.

### Market size (estimates — check)

- **TAM:** Europe has roughly 9–10M people living with dementia (published estimates; verify per source), the majority living at home. Assume 30% have night-time restlessness → ~3M households; at €99 = **~€300M** theoretical.
- **SAM:** households in DE, NL, BE, AT, Nordics with a tech-capable carer: ~15% → 400–500k; **~€45M**.
- **SOM (3 yrs):** **5–15k units** via carer communities, online pharmacies/care shops and home-care providers.

### Competition (1 search)

| Alternative | What it is | Why we still win |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing / carer sleeps lightly | Free | Burn-out |
| Door alarms and chimes | €20–60 | Loud, frightening, react after the door opens |
| Voice-prompt door devices (PIR-triggered recorded-message units sold by assistive-tech shops) | Exists; carers record their own voice | PIR triggers on everyone passing; no direction; often no phone alert |
| Pressure mats / bed-exit sensors with pagers | Telecare-style | Triggers on every bathroom trip; no prompt |
| Telecare systems (professional dementia packages) | Comprehensive, monitored | Subscription, professional install, heavier |
| Smart-home DIY (door contact + speaker + automation) | Possible for enthusiasts | Not for the average carer |

**Wedge:** direction-aware (approach zone at the door, not just motion) + familiar-voice prompt *first* + notification only if ignored. Calm by design.

### Product & value proposition

- **Hardware:** wall/shelf unit facing the hallway; LD2450 tracks up to 3 targets with coordinates → "approach zone" polygon near the door; Grove-MP3-class audio player + small speaker for recorded messages; optional hallway light (built-in soft LED and/or a smart-plug trigger); Tuya CBU Wi-Fi/BLE module for phone notifications; USB-C power + backup cell optional.
- **Behaviour:** night window (e.g. 22:00–06:00); person enters approach zone → message 1 + soft light; still in zone after 30–60 s → message 2; still there or door contact opens (optional add-on) → notify carer. Daytime: silent.
- **Set-up:** carer records 2–3 messages on the phone; draws the approach zone in the app on a live dot map.
- **Pricing (estimates):** €89–119; door-contact add-on €15; care-provider bundles at €69/unit + €2/month if they want a dashboard.

### Go-to-market

**First 100 customers:** national Alzheimer's/dementia association forums and carer support groups (Alzheimer's Society Talking Point-type forums in UK, Alzheimer Nederland and Deutsche Alzheimer Gesellschaft communities — engage respectfully, ask for feedback, don't spam), Facebook carer groups, local carer meetups ("Alzheimer Café" evenings), home-care nurses who see the problem every week.

**Then scale:** assistive-technology retailers and care-supply webshops, home-care providers bundling it, municipalities/social services with assistive-tech budgets, insurers' prevention programmes (after evidence from pilots).

### 12-month plan

- **Q1:** 10-family pilot with hand-built units (kill question: still used after 4 weeks? prompt works ≥50%?); ethics guide with a care professional.
- **Q2:** own PCB, enclosure, Tuya white-label app with zone drawing and voice recording; local-only firmware option; 50-unit beta.
- **Q3:** certification, 1,000 units, launch via 2 care webshops and carer communities.
- **Q4:** care-provider bundle, door-contact add-on, second country.

**Team:** founder (ideally with caring experience — credibility matters), embedded engineer (radar zones + audio), app integrator (Tuya OEM app config, part-time), care advisor (nurse or occupational therapist, paid days), Shenzhen partner for PCBA/enclosure.

**Funding to first 1,000 (estimate):** landed hardware excl. cert €24k + tooling €10k + certification €15k + Tuya app/cloud setup €5k + pilots €5k + 6 months team €40k + ethics/legal €3k = **≈€102k**. Pre-orders (200 × €89) ≈ €18k. Rest: social-innovation or ageing-at-home grants (EU and national programmes exist — search current calls), or an angel from care tech.

### Top risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Effectiveness:** prompt doesn't turn the person around | Pilot measures it; multiple voices/messages; honest marketing ("helps many", not "prevents") |
| **Ethics/legal:** seen as restraint or surveillance | Prompt-only, no locking; consent guidance; involve the person where possible; ethical-use guide |
| **Regulatory:** drifting into medical-device claims | Wording rules (see MDR boundary); legal review of all marketing |
| **Tuya cloud dependency:** notifications stop if Tuya cloud, the account or the app changes; vendor lock-in | Local-only mode (prompt + light work without cloud); Tuya-free firmware path (OpenBeken on the same BK7231N, or switch to an ESP32-C3 with own push service) prepared before scale |
| **GDPR / data residency:** presence patterns of a vulnerable person are sensitive (arguably health-related) data | Minimise: no continuous movement logs in the cloud, only "prompt played / ignored" events; use Tuya's EU data centre region and sign a DPA; clear privacy notice to the carer; DPIA |
| **Technical:** false prompts (carer walking past at night, pets) | Approach-zone polygon + direction + height/speed filters; quiet "carer at home" button |
| **Supply:** LD2450 single source; Tuya module contact-only design files | Firmware abstraction for radar; Tuya modules are widely stocked |

### Certification checklist (EU)

- **MDR boundary (state it clearly):** under the Medical Device Regulation (EU) 2017/745, intended purpose decides. If you market the device for "diagnosing, monitoring or treating dementia" or "preventing wandering in dementia patients" as a clinical function, it can become a medical device (likely software/device Class I or higher → notified body, clinical evaluation — a different business). Stay a **home safety and comfort product**: "a gentle voice prompt and light at the door at night, with an optional notification to a family member". No disease-specific performance claims, no "monitoring patients", no health data analytics. Get the intended-purpose statement reviewed by a regulatory consultant (€1–2k).
- **CE — RED:** EN 300 440 (24 GHz radar), EN 300 328 (Wi-Fi/BLE), EN 301 489-1/-3/-17, EN 62368-1 (incl. audio), EN 62311/62479. Estimate €8–12k.
- **EN 18031-1/-2** (RED cybersecurity, since 1 Aug 2025): internet-connected, processes personal data → both apply; Tuya cloud and app are part of the assessment scope — ask Tuya for their EN 18031 documentation. Budget €3–5k.
- **CRA:** reporting from 11 Sep 2026, full from 11 Dec 2027 — SBOM (incl. Tuya SDK), updates, vulnerability handling.
- **GDPR:** carer = account holder; the person in the home is a data subject; events about a vulnerable person's night movements are sensitive — DPIA, data minimisation, EU region, DPA with Tuya, retention limits.
- **RoHS, REACH, WEEE**, packaging EPR. **Battery Regulation** only if you add a backup cell.
- Accessibility: consider the European Accessibility Act for the app if sold to consumers (scope check).
- **Total estimate:** €13–20k incl. regulatory review.

### Validation toolkit

- **Kill question:** Do 10 carer families still use it after 4 weeks, and does the prompt actually turn the person around at least half the time? Also: will carers accept a Tuya-cloud dependency for alerts? Cheapest experiment: 10 hand-built units in a 4-week pilot with a care professional, counting events only.
- **Interview script (5):**
  1. Tell me about the last night your relative got up and went to the door. What happened, and what did you do?
  2. What have you tried (alarms, locks, mats, cameras)? What did your relative feel about them?
  3. Whose voice would calm them most, and what would that voice say?
  4. Would you want a phone notification every time, or only if the prompt didn't work?
  5. Would you pay €99 for this, and does it matter to you whether it needs an internet/cloud account?
- **Landing page:** Headline: "A calm voice at the door at night." Bullets: "Notices someone heading for the door — not someone walking past." / "Plays your own recorded message and turns on a soft light." / "Lets you know only if it's needed. No cameras."
- **Pass:** ≥7 of 10 pilot families still using it at week 4 with ≥50% prompt success, plus 100 pre-orders (€20 deposit) from carer communities.
