1 · Summary · the one-page version

Summary

  • Problem: Owners of older cats and dogs are often told by their vet to "keep an eye on how much they drink". Nobody can: bowls get topped up, several pets share one, water evaporates. Owners notice a change weeks late, or never.
  • Customer: Cat and dog owners in DE, NL, UK, Nordics (they pay and use), especially owners of senior pets; veterinary clinics that recommend it (they influence and may resell).
  • Product: A slim weighing mat (stainless top, non-slip base) for any bowl up to ~2.5 litres. It separates refills from drinking, subtracts evaporation using temperature, learns a 14-day baseline, flags big changes and an empty bowl, and exports a simple "for your vet" summary. White-label app on the Tuya platform. Wellness information only — no diagnosis.
  • Why now: Pet humanisation and senior-pet care spending; vets want owners to bring data; a load cell plus a cheap Wi-Fi module with a ready white-label app makes it a fast product. The leading connected alternative is a cat-only bowl that needs a hub.
  • The ask: ~€85k to first 1,000 units; the kill question is whether 3 veterinary clinic groups will recommend it — without the vet channel, customer acquisition is too expensive.
2 · Validate demand · before anything else

Validate demand first

Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5). The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

The gate: this idea's kill question

Will 3 veterinary clinic chains agree to recommend it in waiting rooms — and do owners accept a Tuya-cloud app? Without the vet channel, CAC is too high.

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: a measured conversion on a fake-door landing page for the consumer side and, for the business side, at least three decision-makers who put something on paper: a letter of intent, a paid pilot or a purchase order. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

Who you are looking for

The audience named in the idea: Cat and dog owners in DE/NL/UK/Nordics; veterinary clinics recommending it to owners of older pets.

The problem as the pack states it: Owners of older cats and dogs are often told by their vet to "keep an eye on how much they drink". Nobody can: bowls get topped up, several pets share one, water evaporates. Owners notice a change weeks late, or never.

Talk to the people who have the problem and the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

  • r/cats · reddit.com/r/cats/ — cat owners.
  • r/dogs · reddit.com/r/dogs/ — dog owners; health and behaviour questions.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

  • Search for communities around the problem, not the product: forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
  • Trade associations and their regional meetings for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
  • LinkedIn: search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
  • Your deposit list from the kill question and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

  1. 15–20 real conversations (ten buyers from the business side and ten from the consumer side), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
  2. A fake door. One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€59–79 (estimate), the pack's estimate), and one button. Two buttons: a refundable deposit or waitlist for consumers, "request a pilot" for businesses.
  3. Send it to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
  4. Count against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

Thanks for the time. I am looking into how cat and dog owners in DE/NL/UK/Nordics deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. How do you know today how much your cat or dog drinks, and has a change in drinking ever been the first thing you or your vet noticed?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] A weighing mat under the water bowl that learns how much your cat or dog normally drinks and tells you when that changes a lot — plus a 'bowl empty' alert when you're at work. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €59–79 (estimate), and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.

Go / no-go

  • Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit) when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
  • Change the idea when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
  • Stop when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

3 · Business plan · estimates, not a forecast

Business plan

Problem & customer

Drinking more (or less) is one of the things vets ask about, and owners can't answer. A measuring cup and a notebook works for three days. The pet doesn't care; the owner does — mostly the owner of a 10-plus-year-old cat.

  • Who pays: owners of senior cats and dogs, multi-pet households, owners away during working hours. Typically 30–65, already spending on premium food and insurance.
  • Who recommends: vets and vet nurses (waiting-room display, discharge leaflets), pet insurers (wellness perks), breeders.
  • Who uses: the owner, via the app; the pet uses the bowl.
  • Positioning: a "know your pet's normal" tool. Never "detects kidney disease", "diabetes alert" or any condition.

Market size (estimates — check)

  • TAM: EU+UK pet-owning households ≈ 90M+ (FEDIAF Facts & Figures — check latest); cat or dog in ~60–70% → ~60M households. One mat at €69 → ~€4B theoretical.
  • SAM: households with a senior pet (assume 35%) in DE/NL/UK/Nordics (~20M cat/dog households) who buy pet tech (assume 10%) → ~700k → ~€48M.
  • SOM (3 years): 15–25k units (D2C + vet channel + one pet retailer) → €1.0–1.6M. Year 1: 1,000–3,000.

Product & value proposition

  • Mat: ~280×200×18 mm, stainless top plate (dishwasher-safe), ABS base with feet, load cells at the corners, USB-C powered (bowl-side cable with a magnetic break-away, chew-resistant sleeve).
  • Algorithm: detects refill (large step up), drinking events (small step down with pet-on-mat signature), evaporation (slow drift vs temperature and time), spills (sudden drop + noise). Learns a 14-day baseline in ml/day; flags ±30–50% sustained changes (owner-tunable) and "bowl empty/low".
  • Multi-pet: total household intake only in v1. Honest: the mat can't tell cats apart (optional BLE collar tag in v2, or a bowl camera — no, privacy and cost).
  • App: daily ml, 7/30-day trend, notes ("new food"), export a one-page PDF for the vet.
  • Local fallback: LED on the mat (green / amber "check trend" / red "bowl empty"), works without app after setup.

Pricing (estimates): €69 D2C/retail; vet-clinic bundle (display + 6 units) at clinic price; no subscription in v1 (Tuya app has no running cost to the owner).

Go-to-market

First 100 customers:

  • Senior-cat communities: cat forums, Facebook groups for owners of senior cats, cat-health subreddits — offer 20 beta mats in exchange for 30 days of data and honest posts.
  • Five independent vet practices in one city: free display unit + 3 demo mats; vet nurses see the trend data and tell owners.
  • Pet insurers' wellness newsletters (many run partner offers) — pitch as a perk.

Then scale: vet groups (the kill question: three groups recommending), online pet retailers, pet-tech marketplaces, breeders' associations, UK/Nordics rollout.

12-month plan

  • Q1: 15 prototypes in 15 homes (8 cats, 7 dogs) for 4 weeks — accuracy vs a measuring jug; vet interviews (10) and 3 vet-group meetings.
  • Q2: rev A, Tuya app configured, vet summary PDF; 50-unit beta via 5 clinics.
  • Q3: rev B, certification, tooling, 1,000-unit run; D2C launch.
  • Q4: vet-group programme, pet retailer listing, UK launch (UKCA).

Team: founder (vet/pet-trade sales), embedded engineer (part-time; load cells + Tuya SDK), data person (part-time; the drinking-event algorithm is the product), industrial designer (contract; the mat must look good in a kitchen), a veterinary advisor (paid, a few hours/month — for wording and the vet channel), Shenzhen partner.

Certification checklist (EU)

  • CE — RED: EN 300 328 (Wi-Fi/BLE), EN 301 489-1/-17, EN 62368-1 (low-voltage with certified adaptor), EN 62311/EN 62479. Tuya module reports help.
  • EN 18031-1 and -2: internet-connected; processes personal data (account, routine) → both. Est. €3–5k.
  • CRA: product with digital elements + remote data processing (Tuya cloud) → SBOM, OTA, vulnerability handling, supplier terms by Dec 2027.
  • Food contact: the top plate isn't food contact (the bowl is), but if you sell a bowl in the bundle, Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 applies to it.
  • RoHS, REACH, WEEE, packaging registration; Ecodesign rules for the external power supply (buy compliant).
  • GDPR: DPA with Tuya, EU data residency confirmed, privacy notice.
  • MDR boundary: the MDR covers devices for humans; this is not one. But don't drift: no claims to diagnose, monitor or treat any condition in animals; "wellness information to discuss with your vet". Check national veterinary-device rules in your launch countries.
  • No battery (unless you add backup), no toy directive.
  • Estimated test costs: €11–15k.

Validation toolkit

  • Kill question: Will 3 veterinary clinic groups agree to recommend it in waiting rooms — and do owners accept a Tuya-cloud app? Without the vet channel, customer acquisition is too expensive. Cheapest experiment: one working mat, a one-page "what it measures and what it doesn't", and meetings with the clinical directors of 3 practice groups.
  • Interview script (5) — owners: 1. How old is your pet? Has a vet ever asked how much it drinks? What did you answer? 2. How many pets share the water bowl? Bowl or fountain? 3. When did you last notice a change in your pet's habits, and how? 4. Would you use an app for this — and does it matter to you where the data is stored? 5. €69 for a mat that tracks it automatically — buy now, buy if your vet recommended it, or no?
  • Interview script — vets (bonus): what would make you mention it to an owner, and what wording would make you refuse?
  • Landing page: Headline: "Know how much your pet really drinks." Bullets: "Works under the bowl your cat or dog already uses." / "Learns their normal and tells you when it changes a lot." / "A one-page summary to bring to your vet."
  • Pre-order pass: 1 vet group committed + ≥200 D2C pre-orders at €55 within 30 days.

First production checklist

  • Samples: 10 mats; golden sample with calibration curve (0–3 kg) and 7-day drift test at 20 °C and 28 °C.
  • DFM review: load-cell mounting and overload stops (a dog standing on the mat), sealing around the PCB and connector, top plate flatness, feet.
  • MOQ talk: Tuya modules + licences; load cells 4k pcs; stainless plates 1k; base tooling.
  • QC/AQL: ISO 2859-1 level II, AQL 0.65/2.5; 100% calibration with two reference weights, Wi-Fi pairing test, LED test.
  • Packaging: premium box (pet-tech shelf), quick start in DE/NL/EN/SV, vet leaflet.
  • Incoterms: FOB Shenzhen; consolidate with adaptors from the adaptor OEM.
