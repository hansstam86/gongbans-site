2 · Validate demand · before anything else

Validate demand first

Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5). The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

The gate: this idea's kill question

Do 10 individual hobbyist potters (not schools or studios) buy at €299 after seeing one logged firing on a kiln like theirs, and does a kiln repair technician agree to fit it? Pass = 10 orders and 1 installer in 8 weeks.

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: a fake-door landing page that individual hobbyist potters with a small electric kiln at home, kiln repair technicians as installers actually visited, with a measured conversion to a waitlist or refundable deposit, plus the interview notes behind it. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

Who you are looking for

The audience named in the idea: Individual hobbyist potters with a small electric kiln at home (DE/UK/NL/Nordics), kiln repair technicians as installers; explicitly not schools, colleges or commercial studios, where insurance terms rule out non-listed controllers.

The problem as the pack states it: Home potters firing a small electric kiln either babysit a manual kiln-sitter or pay $388–699 for a replacement controller sold through dealers; the ones who want a log and a phone notification have to build it.

Talk to the people who have the problem and the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

  • r/Pottery · reddit.com/r/Pottery/ — hobby potters, many with a home kiln.
  • Digitalfire · digitalfire.com/ — ceramics reference and community; the source on controller safety and insurance.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

  • Search for communities around the problem, not the product: forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
  • Trade associations and their regional meetings for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
  • LinkedIn: search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
  • Your deposit list from the kill question and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

  1. 15–20 real conversations (twenty people from the audience line), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
  2. A fake door. One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€249–349, the pack's estimate), and one button. For a consumer the button takes a refundable deposit or a waitlist e-mail; a deposit is worth ten e-mails.
  3. Send it to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
  4. Count against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

Thanks for the time. I am looking into how individual hobbyist potters with a small electric kiln at home, kiln repair technicians as installers deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. Tell me about your kiln: how do you set a firing, how do you know it is done, and what was your last ruined firing?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] A retrofit controller and logger for the home potter's small electric kiln: ramp-and-hold programmes, a phone notification when the firing is done, and a log of every firing, for individual hobbyists, not schools or studios. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €249–349, and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.

Go / no-go

  • Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit) when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
  • Change the idea when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
  • Stop when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

3 · Business plan · estimates, not a forecast

Business plan

Problem & customer

A kiln firing is a ten-hour ramp-and-hold profile; getting it right decides the result, and knowing it has finished decides whether you sleep. Replacement controllers from Skutt and Bartlett cost $388–699 and are sold and fitted through dealers; the DIY alternatives on forums work but are explicitly not listed for safety, and the Digitalfire community notes that such controllers are implicated in fire-insurance disputes. That settles the market: a safety-certified, installer-fitted controller for the individual hobbyist, scoped away from institutions whose insurers require listed equipment.

Who pays: the hobbyist potter (B2C), typically after the second kiln or the first ruined glaze firing; the kiln repair technician who fits it is the channel. Who does not: schools and studios, by design, until a listed version exists.

Market size (estimates — check)

  • TAM: hobby electric kilns in private hands in the EU: estimate 150,000–300,000. Theoretical €50–90M.
  • SAM: owners of manual or ageing controllers who follow pottery communities online: estimate 50,000 → €15M.
  • SOM (3 yrs): 3,000–5,000 units through communities and technicians; year-1: 400–600.

Product & value proposition

  • Isolated K-type (and S-type option) input; 0–1,300 °C; cone tables; ramp/hold editor on a phone over local WiFi; no cloud.
  • Pilot output (SSR or relay) to the kiln's existing contactor; the controller never carries the element current.
  • Watchdogs: over-temperature, thermocouple break, stuck-relay detection (temperature rising with output off), maximum firing time; mains-independent buzzer.
  • Firing log with the full curve, exportable; notification by e-mail or local push when done or faulted.
  • Installer note and a scope statement in the box: for private hobby kilns; institutions require a listed controller.

4 · Start kit · boards, BOM, build plan, certification

Start kit

Boards to start from

  • M5Stack Unit KMeter-ISO (U133-V11) — isolated K-type thermocouple input for the kiln probe (design files published for the module; electronics not open)
  • M5Stack StamPLC (K141) — DIN-rail PLC-style controller core (design files published for the module; electronics not open)
  • M5Stack StamPLC AC (A160) — mains-powered StamPLC variant (design files published for the module; electronics not open)
  • Seeed Grove-Solid State Relay V2 — pilot-signal SSR reference; the kiln load goes through an external contactor (open files: schematic)
  • M5Stack Unit Buzzer (U132) — end-of-firing and fault alarm (design files published for the module; electronics not open)

The KMeter-ISO is the isolated thermocouple front end; the StamPLC family provides the DIN-rail controller; the Seeed SSR board (open schematic) is the pilot-output reference. The kiln contactor is bought and fitted by the technician.

Certification checklist (EU)

  • Everything in the standard list (RED if radio, EMC, RoHS/REACH, WEEE, CRA) plus LVD / EN 62368-1 or EN 60730-1 for a mains-connected control device; the switched load goes through an installer-fitted contactor or SSR rated for it, never through the box's own relay above its rating.
  • Installer-fitted product: write the installation manual for an electrician, not a consumer; label the box accordingly.
  • Budget the lab at the high end (estimate; get two quotes) and allow one re-spin.

Validation toolkit

  • Kill question: Do 10 individual hobbyist potters (not schools or studios) buy at €299 after seeing one logged firing on a kiln like theirs, and does a kiln repair technician agree to fit it? Pass = 10 orders and 1 installer in 8 weeks.
  • Cheapest experiment: Fit the prototype to one technician's bench kiln, log three firings, and show the log to ten hobbyists with a €299 price; ask who orders.
  • 5-question interview: 1) Tell me about your kiln and your last ruined firing. 2) How do you know when it is done today? 3) Have you priced a replacement controller; what stopped you? 4) Who services your kiln? 5) At €299 fitted by your technician, would you order?
  • Landing page: headline "Your kiln, programmed from the sofa, logged forever. For home potters." Bullets: fitted by your kiln technician; watchdogs; €299; not for schools or studios (listed controllers required). Button: refundable €30 deposit.
  • Pass threshold: 10 hobbyist orders and one technician agreement within 8 weeks.

First production checklist

  • 10 golden samples measured and photographed; one per enclosure colour.
  • DFM review with the PCBA house: test pads, programming jig, serial/MAC provisioning, who holds the signing keys (you).
  • MOQ talk: module, enclosure, packaging; get the next price break in writing.
  • QC: AQL II 2.5/4.0; 100% functional test incl. radio; burn-in sample.
  • Packaging and label: CE, WEEE bin, responsible-person address, model/serial; manual with safety and disposal text.
  • Incoterm and shipping: DDP by courier for the first run; forwarder quote for the second.
  • Safety: 100% hi-pot and functional watchdog test on the line; installer note in every box.
