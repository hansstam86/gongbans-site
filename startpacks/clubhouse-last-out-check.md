# Clubhouse Last-Out Check — Founder Start Pack

> When the last volunteer locks up, one press of a button confirms the clubhouse is empty, doors are shut and the heating and lights are off — and sends the club board one "all good" message, or exactly what was left on.

Concept from the GONGBANS inventor. Not validated. All numbers are estimates — check them. Check every board's licence before commercial use.

## 1. Summary

- **Problem:** Volunteer-run clubhouses are left with the heating on all weekend, lights burning in the changing rooms, a side door unlatched, or someone still in the shower when the front door gets locked. Energy bills went up; volunteers rotate; nobody owns "lock-up".
- **Customer:** Amateur sports clubs (football, hockey, tennis, rowing), scouting groups, village halls run by volunteers. The board/treasurer pays; the rotating "last-out" volunteer uses it.
- **Product:** A set: a mains-powered hub with a 24 GHz multi-target radar (is anyone still inside?), a light sensor, a big "Lock-up" button and a BLE gateway; 4 battery door contacts; 2 energy-metering smart plugs/relays for heaters or boilers. Press Lock-up → 30-second check → green "all good" or a checklist of what's still on/open. Daily energy summary to the board. White-label app on the Tuya platform.
- **Why now:** Club energy bills and volunteer shortages; boards don't want a security-company contract at €30–50/month. Cheap presence radar + Tuya modules + off-the-shelf certified plugs make it a €299 set.
- **The ask:** ~€80k to first 1,000 sets; kill question: will 10 clubs in one federation region buy after a demo at a club-board evening, and still use the button after 2 months?

## 2. Validate demand first

**Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5).** The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

### The gate: this idea's kill question

> Will 10 clubs in one sports federation region buy a set after a demo at a club-board evening, and keep using the lock-up button after 2 months?

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: a measured conversion on a fake-door landing page for the consumer side and, for the business side, at least three decision-makers who put something on paper: a letter of intent, a paid pilot or a purchase order. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

### Who you are looking for

**The audience named in the idea:** Amateur football, hockey, tennis and rowing clubs; scouting groups; village halls run by volunteers.

**The problem as the pack states it:** Volunteer-run clubhouses are left with the heating on all weekend, lights burning in the changing rooms, a side door unlatched, or someone still in the shower when the front door gets locked. Energy bills went up; volunteers rotate; nobody owns "lock-up".

Talk to the people who have the problem *and* the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

### Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

- **DOSB (DE)** · https://www.dosb.de/ — German sports confederation; the regional Landessportbund reaches the clubs.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

- **Search for communities around the problem, not the product:** forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
- **Trade associations and their regional meetings** for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
- **LinkedIn:** search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
- **Your deposit list from the kill question** and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

### The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

1. **15–20 real conversations** (ten buyers from the business side and ten from the consumer side), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
2. **A fake door.** One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€249–349 set (estimate), the pack's estimate), and one button. Two buttons: a refundable deposit or waitlist for consumers, "request a pilot" for businesses.
3. **Send it** to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
4. **Count** against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

### Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

```email
Thanks for the time. I am looking into how amateur football, hockey, tennis and rowing clubs deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. Tell me about the last time the clubhouse was left with a door open, lights on or the heating running overnight: who found out, when, and what did it cost the club?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] When the last volunteer locks up, one sensor set confirms the clubhouse is empty, doors are shut and the heating and lights are off — and sends the board a single 'all good' message, or exactly what was left on. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €249–349 set (estimate), and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.
```

### Reading the signal

| Real signal | False positive |
|---|---|
| A refundable deposit, a signed LOI, a paid pilot, a calendar invite for the prototype | "Sounds cool", "I would totally buy that", likes and upvotes |
| They describe the problem unprompted and in detail, with numbers | They agree with your description of the problem |
| They have already paid for a workaround (a product, a service, their own time) | They have never tried anything and are not bothered |
| Landing-page conversion to deposit or form above the pack's pass mark, from cold traffic | Conversion from friends, family and your own network |
| A named budget and a named decision-maker (B2B) | An enthusiastic user who cannot buy |

### Go / no-go

- **Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit)** when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
- **Change the idea** when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
- **Stop** when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

## 3. Business plan

### Problem & customer

German sports federations count ~86,000 clubs (DOSB, 2025 survey). Many own or run a clubhouse with changing rooms, a bar, a kitchen and a boiler. The last-out routine lives on a laminated sheet by the door. One weekend of heating left on in winter can cost €100–300 (estimate); a door left open invites theft and vandalism and can be an insurance argument.

- **Who pays:** club treasurer/board (budget line "facilities" or "energy"), sometimes municipalities that own the building and let it to clubs, and village-hall committees.
- **Who uses:** rotating volunteers (trainers, bar staff, parents). They will not install an app. The button must work without one; the app is for the facilities volunteer and the board.
- **Who influences:** regional sports federations (Landessportbünde) that run energy advice and funding programmes for clubs (check current programmes), club insurers.

### Market size (estimates — check)

- **TAM:** DE ~86k sports clubs; assume 40% run a clubhouse → ~34k. Add NL/BE/AT/UK clubhouses and village halls ≈ another ~40k. ~75k buildings × €299 = **~€22M** hardware.
- **SAM:** clubhouses with paid heating/energy and a board that buys tech: assume 30% → 22k → **~€6.7M**.
- **SOM (3 years):** 4–5% of SAM → **~1,000 sets**, ~€0.3M, plus optional summary service. Small. This is a federation-partnership business or a feature for a building-tech company. Go in with eyes open.

### Competition (1 search + category knowledge)

| Alternative | What it is | Why we can still win |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing / laminated checklist | Free | Depends on the most tired volunteer of the evening |
| Security company alarm contract | Arming, intrusion detection, monitoring | €30–50/month (estimate); doesn't check heating/lights; arming fails if someone's still inside — ours tells you who/where first |
| Consumer smart home (Tuya/Smart Life, Shelly, Homey, Home Assistant) | Can do all of it | Needs a club member who's a smart-home enthusiast — and leaves when they leave the club |
| Smart thermostats/timer switches | Heating schedules | Solves one item; no "is anyone still inside", no door check |
| Building management systems | For big sports centres | Overkill and price |

**Wedge:** one button, one message, works for whoever is last tonight. Sold through the federation channel at a club-board evening, not through an app store.

### Product & value proposition

- **Hub:** wall unit near the main door, USB-C powered (certified adaptor), HLK-LD2460 radar covering the main room (static ≥5 m / moving ≥6 m per module spec), light sensor, big illuminated button, buzzer, BLE gateway for the door contacts, Wi-Fi to the cloud.
- **Coverage honesty:** one radar covers one room. Changing rooms and showers need an extra radar node (€59) each. A typical clubhouse = hub + 2 nodes.
- **Door contacts:** BLE, coin cell, reed switch — they report closed/open, not locked. For "locked" add a bolt sensor in the strike plate (later SKU).
- **Energy:** two off-the-shelf, certified Tuya energy-metering plugs/relays (bought in, same app) for heaters, water boiler or the bar fridge; light sensor in each radar unit tells you lights are on.
- **Lock-up flow:** press → hub checks presence (all radars quiet for 30 s), doors closed, plugs in "night" state, lights off → green + "all good" to the board's group; or red + spoken/visual list ("Changing room 2: light on, someone present").
- **Board report:** daily kWh on the metered loads, number of lock-ups, exceptions.

**Pricing (estimates):** starter set (hub + 4 contacts + 2 plugs) **€299**; extra radar node €59; extra contacts €19. Optional board e-mail report service €3/month (needs our own backend — or keep it free inside the Tuya app).

### Go-to-market

**First 100 customers:**
- One regional sports federation or district football association: ask to present at a club-board evening on energy savings (they run these). Bring a live demo in a box.
- Your own club network: the first 10 clubs are friends-of-friends. Offer install by you, in person.
- Village-hall committees via rural community associations; scouting groups' regional offices.
- Club-management software newsletters and club-treasurer communities (e.g. Facebook groups for Vereinsvorstände).

**Then scale:** framework deal with a federation ("recommended by"), club insurers (fewer claims), municipal sports offices that own clubhouses, then NL/BE via their sports federations. Partner with club-management software (integration later).

### 12-month plan

- **Q1:** demo kit built from dev boards + off-the-shelf Tuya plugs; 2 club-board evenings; 10 interviews with treasurers and facilities volunteers.
- **Q2:** 10 pilot clubs (paid at €199 pilot price), rev A hub + contacts; measure button usage weekly.
- **Q3:** rev B, certification, 1,000-set run (or 300 if pilots are lukewarm — be honest); federation partnership talks.
- **Q4:** second federation, extra radar node SKU, winter energy story for the board report.

**Team:** founder (sales in person — this is a handshake market), embedded engineer (part-time; Tuya SDK + radar), installer/support person (part-time, volunteers need hand-holding the first week), Shenzhen partner.

**Funding to first 1,000 sets (estimate):** landed hardware excl. cert (1,000 × €60.8) €61k + tooling €12k + certification €18k + Tuya setup €3k + prototypes/pilots €5k + 6 months part-time team €30k + demo kits/travel €5k = **≈€134k**. That's too much for this market size. Start with **300 sets**: €18k hardware + €12k tooling + €18k cert + €3k + €5k + €30k + €5k = **≈€91k**, offset by pilot revenue (10 × €199) and pre-paid orders from a federation deal (€20–40k). Route: bootstrap + federation pre-orders; club-energy grants that clubs can use to buy it (check Landessportbund and state programmes — this is the real lever: if a grant pays the club, the sale is easy); no VC.

### Top risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Technical:** radar sees through thin walls/doors and reports "someone present" from outside | Range gates, mounting guide, per-room calibration at install; LD2460 trajectory data to ignore static clutter |
| **Technical:** BLE contacts out of hub range in long buildings | Radar nodes double as BLE relays; site check at install |
| **Market:** volunteers stop pressing the button after week 3 | The hub nags at closing time (presence drops → doors still open → buzzer); board sees "no lock-up" days; that social pressure is the product |
| **Platform dependency (Tuya cloud):** board messages, app and remote status depend on Tuya's cloud; if the account ends, the app stops | Local-first: the button, the check and the hub's red/green work without the cloud. Disclose cloud dependency to the club in the offer. Contract terms with Tuya in writing |
| **Tuya-free alternative** | Same hub PCB with an ESP32-C3/C6 footprint → ESPHome/Home Assistant or Matter build for clubs with an IT volunteer, and your own small backend for the board e-mail. More work, no platform lock-in |
| **GDPR / data residency:** presence logs and lock-up times can be linked to named volunteers (and to staff, where clubs employ bar staff → employee monitoring rules) | Don't log names by default; aggregate presence only; Tuya EU data centre and DPA confirmed in writing; privacy notice for the club; no cameras ever |
| **Supply:** Hi-Link radar + Tuya modules single source | Alternate radar footprint in rev B; buy plugs from two Tuya-ecosystem brands |

### Certification checklist (EU)

- **CE — RED** for hub, radar node and contacts: EN 300 440 (24 GHz), EN 300 328 (Wi-Fi/BLE), EN 301 489-1/-3/-17, EN 62368-1, EN 62311/EN 62479. Bought-in plugs carry their own CE — check their DoC and keep it in your file.
- **EN 18031-1 / -2:** hub is internet-connected; processes personal data (accounts, presence) → -2 as well. Est. €3–5k.
- **CRA:** system with cloud backend (Tuya) → SBOM, OTA updates, vulnerability handling by Dec 2027; supplier obligations in the Tuya contract.
- **RoHS, REACH, WEEE**, packaging.
- **Battery Regulation:** coin cells supplied in contacts → registration, labelling.
- **GDPR:** club is the controller; you (and Tuya) are processors → DPA template, privacy notice text for the clubhouse wall, EU data residency.
- No MDR, no toy directive. Not an intrusion alarm — don't market it as security equipment (EN 50131 would apply to alarm systems).
- **Estimated test costs:** €15–20k for hub + node + contacts.

### Validation toolkit

- **Kill question:** Will 10 clubs in one federation region buy a set after a demo at a club-board evening — and still use the lock-up button after 2 months? **Cheapest experiment:** one demo box, one board evening, a sign-up sheet with "pilot €199, pay now".
- **Interview script (5) — treasurers/facilities volunteers:**
  1. Who locks up, and how do they know everything is off?
  2. When did something get left on or open last? What did it cost?
  3. What do you pay for energy and security today?
  4. Would your volunteers press a button at the door? What would make them skip it?
  5. Would you pay €299 for the set if it stops one weekend of heating per winter? Who signs off?
- **Landing page:** Headline: "Last one out? One button tells the board everything's off." Bullets: "Checks nobody's inside, doors shut, heating and lights off." / "Works for whoever locks up tonight — no app needed." / "Daily energy summary for the treasurer."
- **Pre-order pass:** 10 clubs pay a €199 pilot price within 6 weeks of one federation evening.

### First production checklist

- **Samples:** 5 hubs, 20 contacts; golden samples with radar detection at 5 m (static person) documented.
- **DFM review:** hub button durability (50k presses), contact housing adhesive on painted doors/metal frames, magnet gap tolerance.
- **MOQ talk:** Tuya modules + licences, radar modules 500–1k, plugs from a Tuya-ecosystem OEM (MOQ 500).
- **QC/AQL:** ISO 2859-1 level II, AQL 0.65/2.5; 100% hub test (radar detect, button, Wi-Fi + BLE pairing), contacts tested in batch with a test hub.
- **Packaging:** "install in one hour" guide with a floor-plan sheet; labels for each contact (front door, side door…).
- **Incoterms:** FOB Shenzhen; plugs shipped direct from their OEM to your EU warehouse or consolidated in Shenzhen.
