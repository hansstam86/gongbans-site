1 · Summary · the one-page version

Summary

  • Problem: Kindergarten and classroom noise regularly reaches levels that damage teachers' hearing and children's concentration; staff know it but have no live feedback, and the institutional noise lights cost €1,200–3,500 each.
  • Customer: Kindergarten operators (municipal, church and private Träger running 5–200 sites), primary schools, libraries and open-plan offices. The Träger's facility or health-and-safety lead pays; the teacher uses it.
  • Product: A wall-mounted traffic light with a microphone, a live colour (green / amber / red with adjustable thresholds), a daily exposure log readable on the device and exportable over WiFi, powered by USB-C. Target price €99–149 (estimate).
  • Why now: Noise in kindergartens is a recognised occupational hazard (DGUV / Unfallkassen guidance), the institutional product costs over €1,200, and lending programmes exist precisely because nobody can afford to buy one per room.
  • The ask: ~€35–45k to certification and a first 1,000 units (estimate); one Träger framework order covers half.

External market check (November 2026): LIKELY (STRONG). SoundEar 3 sells at €1,230–3,500 to institutions, a far larger gap than assumed. German DGUV / Unfallkassen already recognise kindergarten noise as a hazard and run free noise-light lending programmes. Main risk: The free lending programmes are a competing alternative for the trial phase; the product must be the purchase after the loan. 2 · Validate demand · before anything else

Validate demand first

Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5). The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

The gate: this idea's kill question

Do 10 kindergartens of one Träger buy at €119 after a two-week trial unit, knowing that their Unfallkasse lends similar lights for free? Pass = 10 orders or one Träger-wide framework order.

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: written answers from at least ten decision-makers who hold the budget, at least three of whom put something on paper: a signed letter of intent, a paid pilot, or a dated purchase order for the first units. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

Who you are looking for

The audience named in the idea: Kindergartens and primary schools (teacher hearing-protection guidance from the German Unfallkassen), libraries, open-plan offices; kindergarten operators (Träger) as the buyer.

The problem as the pack states it: Kindergarten and classroom noise regularly reaches levels that damage teachers' hearing and children's concentration; staff know it but have no live feedback, and the institutional noise lights cost €1,200–3,500 each.

Talk to the people who have the problem and the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

  • r/ECEProfessionals · reddit.com/r/ECEProfessionals/ — early-childhood educators; noise and stress come up often.
  • r/Teachers · reddit.com/r/Teachers/ — teachers in general; use for the budget-holder question.
  • Tes Community (UK) · community.tes.com/ — UK teachers' forum.
  • GEFMA · gefma.de/ — German facility-management association.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

  • Search for communities around the problem, not the product: forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
  • Trade associations and their regional meetings for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
  • LinkedIn: search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
  • Your deposit list from the kill question and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

  1. 15–20 real conversations (ten budget-holders and ten users), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
  2. A fake door. One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€99–149, the pack's estimate), and one button. For a business buyer the button is "request a pilot" that leads to a short form and a letter of intent.
  3. Send it to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
  4. Count against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

Thanks for the time. I am looking into how kindergartens and primary schools, libraries, open-plan offices deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. How loud does it get in your rooms on a bad day, how do you know, and what did you do about it last time?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] A wall traffic light that shows classroom or kindergarten noise level live and logs daily exposure for the teacher, with no subscription and no app required. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €99–149, and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.

Pre-order / pilot message (forum post or direct message)

Subject: Noise Light: looking for 3 pilot sites before we build it

I am [your name], [your background]. We are considering building a Noise Light: A wall traffic light that shows classroom or kindergarten noise level live and logs daily exposure for the teacher, with no subscription and no app required. Before we spend money on tooling I want to find out whether this solves a real problem for kindergartens and primary schools, libraries, open-plan offices.

If it would, I am looking for three pilot customers willing to sign a short letter of intent now and pay €99–149 per unit for the first batch, delivered in [month]. The LOI is non-binding on the price; it tells me the problem is real.

Two questions, no pitch: how do you handle this today, and what would make you say no to the above? Twenty minutes on a call this week, or a reply here, both help.

Go / no-go

  • Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit) when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
  • Change the idea when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
  • Stop when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

3 · Business plan · estimates, not a forecast

Business plan

Problem & customer

German accident insurers (Unfallkassen, DGUV) treat kindergarten noise as an occupational hazard and publish guidance on it; they even lend noise lights to sites for a few weeks because the purchase price is prohibitive. A traffic light children can see changes behaviour in the room within minutes; the exposure log gives the teacher an argument for acoustic measures. Today both come in a €1,230–3,500 institutional device or in a web app on a laptop nobody has in the room.

Who pays, B2B (primary): kindergarten Träger and school boards buying for many rooms; libraries and offices as a second segment. Who uses: the teacher, who must be able to set thresholds with two buttons. Free alternative to beat: the Unfallkasse lending programme (a few weeks per site, then it is gone).

Market size (estimates — check)

  • TAM: ~60,000 kindergartens in Germany alone, ~300,000 rooms; plus schools, libraries, offices across the EU. Theoretical €40–60M in DE.
  • SAM: Träger with more than ten sites and a health-and-safety lead: estimate 15,000 rooms addressable per year → €1.5–2M.
  • SOM (3 yrs): 5–10 Träger framework orders plus retail: 6,000–10,000 units. Year-1: 1,000–2,000.

Product & value proposition

  • 150 mm three-colour light, visible from the back of the room; thresholds set by two buttons (default 70/80 dB(A) for kindergartens).
  • Indicative A-weighted level from the MEMS microphone; a calibration tone procedure in the manual; clearly labelled "not a class-2 sound-level meter".
  • Daily exposure log (minutes in amber and red) on a small e-paper or OLED; CSV export over WiFi; no cloud account.
  • Optional quiet-time mode (light off, log on) for nap time.

Firmware/software starting point

  • ESP-IDF; I2S microphone, A-weighting filter, 1-second Leq, hysteresis on colour changes; log per day in flash.
  • Local web page over WiFi (optional) for CSV export; no cloud.
  • Calibration-tone routine with a phone app or a reference meter in the manual.

Certification checklist (EU)

  • RED via the module's own radio reports (EN 300 328 / EN 300 220 as applicable, EN 301 489 EMC), EN 18031 cybersecurity for the connected variant, EN 62368-1 safety (USB-powered, no mains inside), RoHS/REACH, WEEE registration per country, CRA vulnerability-handling process.
  • Test with the DVT build in the real enclosure; budget a re-spin for EMC.
  • Lab cost: a few thousand euros for a USB-powered device with a pre-certified module, more with battery or multiple radios (estimate; get two quotes).

Validation toolkit

  • Kill question: Do 10 kindergartens of one Träger buy at €119 after a two-week trial unit, knowing that their Unfallkasse lends similar lights for free? Pass = 10 orders or one Träger-wide framework order.
  • Cheapest experiment: Three prototypes in three rooms of one kindergarten for two weeks; ask the Träger's safety lead whether they would buy 20 at €119.
  • 5-question interview: 1) How loud does it get in your rooms and how do you know? 2) Have you borrowed a noise light from the Unfallkasse; what happened when it went back? 3) Who decides on purchases like this and what budget line? 4) What would the log need to show to be useful to you? 5) At €119 per room, how many rooms?
  • Landing page: headline "The noise light your Träger can afford." Bullets: see the noise, log the day, €119 instead of €1,200+. Button: request a trial unit (B2B form).
  • Pass threshold: 10 orders or one Träger framework order within 8 weeks of the trials.

First production checklist

  • 10 golden samples measured and photographed; one per enclosure colour.
  • DFM review with the PCBA house: test pads, programming jig, serial/MAC provisioning, who holds the signing keys (you).
  • MOQ talk: module, enclosure, packaging; get the next price break in writing.
  • QC: AQL II 2.5/4.0; 100% functional test incl. radio; burn-in sample.
  • Packaging and label: CE, WEEE bin, responsible-person address, model/serial; manual with safety and disposal text.
  • Incoterm and shipping: DDP by courier for the first run; forwarder quote for the second.
