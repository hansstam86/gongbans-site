A shelf sensor that tells you how your dog spends the day when you're out: how long it paced, rested at the door or barked. It uses radar and a sound-level meter instead of a camera filming your living room.

1 · Summary · the one-page version

Summary

  • Problem: Millions of dogs adopted during the pandemic now spend 4–9 hours alone as owners return to the office. Some cope and some pace, howl and wreck the door. Owners don't know which, and neighbours complain before the owner finds out. Pet cameras give you clips you have to watch, and many people don't want a camera filming their home all day.
  • Customer: Dog owners in hybrid work (DE, NL, UK, FR, Nordics) pay and use it. Dog trainers and behaviourists working on separation problems use the weekly report and recommend the product, so they're the channel. Some landlords have pet clauses, but that's a sensitive segment (see risks).
  • Product: A small shelf/wall unit with a 24 GHz trajectory radar (where the dog moves: rest, pace, at-door) and a sound-level sensor (bark/howl minutes, no audio stored). It shows a day timeline and weekly trend in an app, with an optional "barking >5 min" push.
  • Why now: The separation-anxiety market is real and talked about. Trajectory radar modules that track up to 3 targets cost a few euros. Off-the-shelf white-label smart-home platforms (Tuya) make a phone app possible in weeks, not months.
  • The ask: About €110k (estimate) to reach 1,000 units. Route: trainer-led pre-orders, crowdfunding, then a pet-industry angel.
2 · Validate demand · before anything else

Validate demand first

Do this before anything else in this pack: before the business plan, before you order a sample board, and long before you e-mail a factory (section 5). The classic first-time mistake is to source and build first and find out later that nobody wanted it. Hardware mistakes are slow and expensive to undo; 15–20 conversations are cheap.

The gate: this idea's kill question

Can the radar separate a 10 kg dog's pacing from resting reliably? Test with 10 dogs of different sizes; then ask 20 dog trainers whether they'd recommend it.

You pass this section when you can answer that question with evidence you collected yourself, not a guess: a measured conversion on a fake-door landing page for the consumer side and, for the business side, at least three decision-makers who put something on paper: a letter of intent, a paid pilot or a purchase order. Until then: no factory e-mail, no sample order, no firmware, no logo. If the answer is no, you have lost a few weeks and saved the rest.

Who you are looking for

The audience named in the idea: Dog owners returning to hybrid office work, dog trainers and behaviourists working on separation issues, landlords with pet clauses.

The problem as the pack states it: Millions of dogs adopted during the pandemic now spend 4–9 hours alone as owners return to the office. Some cope and some pace, howl and wreck the door. Owners don't know which, and neighbours complain before the owner finds out. Pet cameras give you clips you have to watch, and many people don't want a camera filming their home all day.

Talk to the people who have the problem and the people who pay; in B2B they are often not the same person, and the one who pays decides.

Where to find them

Communities and associations where this audience actually talks, as far as we know them in November 2026. Confirm each is still active and read its rules on self-promotion before posting; most allow a genuine question about a problem and ban a pitch.

  • r/dogs · reddit.com/r/dogs/ — dog owners; health and behaviour questions.
  • r/Dogtraining · reddit.com/r/Dogtraining/ — owners and trainers; separation problems are a constant topic.

Beyond the named ones, the same four methods work for every idea on this site:

  • Search for communities around the problem, not the product: forums, Facebook groups, Discord servers and newsletters in your own country for the terms in the audience line above. Many of the best groups are national and in the local language; a Dutch or German group beats a global English one for an EU launch.
  • Trade associations and their regional meetings for the B2B audiences: one meeting puts twenty buyers in a room, and the association newsletter will often run a short survey for a member.
  • LinkedIn: search people by the job titles in the audience line, message ten a day with one question (not a pitch), and ask for twenty minutes. Expect one in five to say yes.
  • Your deposit list from the kill question and anyone who replied to the fake door: those are the first interviews, not the last.

The plan: 15–20 conversations, then a fake door

  1. 15–20 real conversations (ten buyers from the business side and ten from the consumer side), fifteen minutes each, using the script below. Before writing a line of code or ordering a board. Write up each one the same day: the problem in their words, what they do today, what it costs them, what they said when you named the price.
  2. A fake door. One page: the pitch as the headline, a rendered image or a photo of a cardboard mock-up, the price (€79–99 (estimate), the pack's estimate), and one button. Two buttons: a refundable deposit or waitlist for consumers, "request a pilot" for businesses.
  3. Send it to everyone you interviewed and post it, with the problem question first, in the communities above. Run it for two to four weeks.
  4. Count against the kill question. Then decide: go, change the idea, or stop.

Interview script (fifteen minutes, problem first)

Ask, then be quiet. Do not describe the product until question 5; if you pitch first you will only hear politeness.

Thanks for the time. I am looking into how dog owners returning to hybrid office work, dog trainers and behaviourists working on separation issues, landlords with pet clauses deal with [the problem in their words]; I have not built anything yet and I am trying to find out whether it is worth building.

1. What do you know about what your dog does while you are out, how did you find out, and what have you tried to find out more?
2. What do you do about it today, and what does that cost you in money or time per month?
3. What have you already tried or bought, and why did you stop using it?
4. If this went away tomorrow, what would change for you? (If the answer is "not much", you have your answer.)
5. [Only now describe it in one sentence:] A shelf sensor that tells you how your dog spends the day when you're out — how long it paced, rested at the door or barked — with radar and a sound-level meter instead of a camera filming your living room. On a scale of one to ten, how likely would you be to buy that at €79–99 (estimate), and what would make it a ten?
6. Who else should I talk to about this? (Ask for two names.)

Close: "If I build this, can I come back to you with a prototype?" A yes with a calendar date is worth more than any compliment.

Pre-order / pilot message (forum post or direct message)

Subject: Dog Alone-Time Logger: would you pay €79–99 (estimate) for this? Looking for 20 people to tell me I am wrong

I am [your name], [your background]. I am considering building a Dog Alone-Time Logger: A shelf sensor that tells you how your dog spends the day when you're out — how long it paced, rested at the door or barked — with radar and a sound-level meter instead of a camera filming your living room. Before I order a single part I want to know whether this is a real problem for people like you or only in my head.

If you would want one at €79–99 (estimate), the page at [link] takes a fully refundable €[10–20] deposit (or a waitlist e-mail if you prefer); if twenty people do that, I build it, if not, everyone gets their money back and I have learned something.

Either way, two questions: how do you deal with this today, and what would make you say no? Honest answers are worth more to me than deposits.

Go / no-go

  • Go to section 3 (business plan) and section 4 (start kit) when the kill question above is met by your own numbers, you have 15–20 written-up conversations, and at least one real signal from the left column per segment you plan to sell to. Only then does section 5 (Who to contact) apply.
  • Change the idea when the problem is real but the price, the buyer or the form factor in the pitch is wrong: the interviews will tell you which. Rewrite the pitch and run the fake door again; this is normal and costs a week.
  • Stop when the kill question fails on honest numbers, or when nobody you talked to has paid for a workaround. Pick another idea on this site; the boards in the start kit are not wasted, only unused.

3 · Business plan · estimates, not a forecast

Business plan

Problem & customer

Primary: owners of 1–3-year-old dogs (pandemic and post-pandemic adoptions) in cities with hybrid work, who suspect a problem: a neighbour note, a chewed door frame, a dog that's "too excited" when they come home. They'll spend €100 to know, and more to fix.

Channel and power user: certified dog trainers and behaviourists. Separation training means gradually extending alone time and checking that the dog stays calm. Today they ask owners to film the dog and send videos. A radar timeline gives them data without watching hours of video. Each trainer sees 20–100 separation cases a year.

Market size (estimates)

  • EU + UK pet dogs: roughly 100 M (FEDIAF-type industry estimates; check the current figure). Assume 30% live with owners who leave them alone ≥4 h on workdays, and 10% of those owners worry enough to buy a tool → ~3 M potential buyers.
  • TAM: 3 M × €89 ≈ €270 M.
  • SAM: DE, NL, UK, FR, Nordics, with an app in their language and online pet-shop reach, ~40% → €110 M.
  • SOM (3 years): 0.5% of SAM → ~6,000 units → ~€530k + trainer subscriptions.

Wedge: a summary, not surveillance: "Today: 3 h 10 rest, 22 min pacing, 9 min at the door, 4 min barking (14:02–14:06)". No camera, no audio recording, and a trainer report you can share. One-off price, no mandatory subscription.

Product & value proposition

  • Radar: Hi-Link LD2450 24 GHz trajectory tracking (up to 3 targets, 6 m, ±60°, 0.75 m resolution, 5 V ~120 mA, UART, per the Hi-Link brochure). Mounted on a shelf or wall at ~1 m height to see the floor zone. The owner draws zones in the app: "bed", "door", "window".
  • Sound: Seeed Grove Sound Sensor class (microphone + amplifier → envelope) for loudness-over-time. Better for production: a MEMS mic with on-device level and bark-pattern classification (energy + periodicity) on the MCU. Raw audio never leaves RAM and is never stored.
  • Connectivity + app: Tuya WBR3 (Wi-Fi + BLE, per Tuya docs) with Tuya's white-label app framework for the first version. A Tuya-free variant is described below.
  • Outputs: day timeline, weekly trend, "alone-time score", trainer PDF/share link, optional real-time push "barking > 5 min".
  • Power: USB-C (the radar draws ~120 mA continuously, so no battery version).

Pricing (estimates): €89 RRP consumer, €69 for trainer-referred clients (trainer gets €10 commission), trainer dashboard €9/month for multi-client views. Pet retailers at 35–40% off.

Go-to-market

First 100 customers:

  • 20 dog trainers/behaviourists in one country: a free unit and dashboard in exchange for using it with 3 clients each. That's 60 owners in 2 months, with case studies.
  • Dog-owner communities: city dog-walking groups, breed clubs (border collies, Malinois, Vizslas — the pacers), Reddit/Facebook separation-anxiety groups.
  • Dog daycare and dog-walker businesses: they hear "how does my dog cope when alone?" every day.
  • Content: a "what my dog really does all day" timeline series with 10 dogs. That's the shareable asset.

Scale channels: online pet retailers (Zooplus-type, Fressnapf-type chains), pet insurers (welfare/prevention programmes), dog schools, vets as a wellness/behaviour recommendation (no medical claims).

For a lean design, run the radar parsing and activity logic on the Tuya module itself (Tuya's TuyaOS SDK on the WBR3) or on the ESP32 in the Tuya-free variant, and drop the extra MCU.

Engineering gap: the sensors and the connectivity exist. What's missing: activity classification (rest / pace / at-door) from x/y tracks, zone set-up UX, bark detection that survives a TV, the daily summary logic, the trainer report, and the app screens. The data science and the UX are the product.

Firmware/software starting point

  • Tuya path: TuyaOS / Tuya IoT development platform (search "Tuya IoT platform product development"). Define data points (DPs) for activity minutes, bark minutes, events; use a Tuya app panel or the white-label OEM app.
  • Tuya-free path: ESP-IDF on an ESP32-C3/C6; ESPHome has an LD2450 component (search "ESPHome LD2450"), which is useful for fast prototyping with Home Assistant. Own backend: MQTT + a small web app; push via standard mobile push services.
  • Radar data: Hi-Link's LD2450 serial protocol document (from the maker); community parsers exist (search "LD2450 Arduino").
  • Audio: compute RMS in 100 ms windows on the MCU; never buffer more than a second.

Certification checklist (EU)

  • CE under RED: 24 GHz radar (module's standard per Hi-Link, e.g. EN 300 440 for 24 GHz short-range devices; confirm), Wi-Fi/BLE EN 300 328, EMC EN 301 489-1/-3/-17, safety EN 62368-1 (low voltage, use a certified USB adapter or ship without one), exposure EN 62311/62479. Est. €8–14k.
  • RED delegated act cybersecurity: EN 18031-1 + EN 18031-2 (internet-connected, processes personal data: presence patterns and a microphone). Mandatory since 1 Aug 2025. If you use Tuya, ask Tuya for its EN 18031 support documentation for the module + cloud; you're still the manufacturer. Est. €8–15k.
  • CRA: reporting from 11 Sept 2026, full from 11 Dec 2027. Security updates, SBOM (including Tuya SDK components), vulnerability handling.
  • GDPR: see risks. DPIA recommended (microphone + home presence). On-device audio processing, clear LED when sensing, an EU data region, and DPAs with the cloud provider.
  • RoHS, REACH, WEEE, packaging registration.
  • No battery, so no Battery Regulation duties unless you add one.
  • Animal claims: wellness/behaviour information only; don't claim to diagnose or treat anxiety (veterinary medicine rules apply to treatment claims in some countries).
  • Total certification budget estimate: €18–30k.

Validation toolkit

  • Kill question: Can the radar separate a 10 kg dog's pacing from resting reliably? Test with 10 dogs of different sizes, then ask 20 dog trainers whether they'd recommend it.
  • Cheapest experiment (~€500): 3 works-like rigs rotated through 10 homes for 3 days each, a phone filming for one day as ground truth, and a hand-made timeline PDF sent to the owner and their trainer. Ask: "Would you pay €89 for this every day?"
  • 5-question interview (trainer): 1) How do you know how a client's dog copes alone today? 2) How much time do you spend watching client videos? 3) Which numbers would change your training plan (pacing, door time, barking onset)? 4) Would you recommend a device to clients, and what would make you refuse? 5) Would you pay €9/month for a multi-client dashboard?
  • Landing page headline: "What does your dog do all day? Find out, without a camera." Bullets: "A daily timeline: rest, pacing, time at the door, barking." / "No camera, no audio recording. Just the summary." / "Share the weekly report with your trainer."
  • Pass threshold: 15 of 20 trainers willing to recommend it and 200 owner deposits (€10) in 6 weeks.

First production checklist

  • Samples: golden sample with radar firmware version and the classification thresholds frozen; test recordings of 3 reference "dog" movements (a rolling RC target works as a jig).
  • DFM review: radar window (no metal, uniform wall thickness), mic port acoustics, USB-C strain relief.
  • MOQ talk: radar module MOQ; Tuya module + platform package (licence fees per device, app fees); enclosure finish (wood-look film has MOQs).
  • QC/AQL: AQL II 1.0/2.5; 100% radar presence test (target at 2 m), 100% Wi-Fi pairing + cloud activation, 100% mic level test with a reference tone.
  • Packaging: retail box with a dog-and-timeline visual, USB-C cable, wall mount, privacy card ("what we measure, what we don't").
  • Incoterms: FOB Shenzhen; check that Tuya device licences are activated per unit (factory token flashing is part of production).
