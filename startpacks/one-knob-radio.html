1 · Summary · the one-page version

Summary

  • Problem: People with dementia stop using radios and music players when the buttons stop making sense; families watch a parent lose the one thing that calmed them. The simple players that exist cost €100–250 and look like hospital equipment.
  • Customer: Families (B2C) and care homes (B2B, 10–50 units) in DE/NL/UK/Nordics; dementia-care retailers and occupational therapists recommend. 9.07 M people live with dementia in the EU27 today (Alzheimer Europe, 2025), 14.3 M by 2050.
  • Product: a wooden radio with one big knob: turn it, it plays — a preset station or the family's playlist from an SD card; turn it off, it stops. Nothing else on the front. Carer setup via SD card or a web page. €139–169.
  • Why now: The sub-€5 Bluetooth-speaker gongban economy gives you the amp + playback; the open four-band radio gives a "real radio" SKU with no code; the work is industrial design, not electronics.
  • The ask: ~€55k to the first 1,000 units; care-home pilots and pre-orders fund a third.
2 · Business plan · estimates, not a forecast

Business plan

Problem & customer

Music is one of the few things that reliably reaches people with moderate dementia, and the carers know it. The incumbents prove the market: the one-button radios and simple music players sell at £50–200 through dementia charities' shops and care catalogues, and a US brand sells a music player + radio at US$200. What they lack is beauty (they look like aids), a playlist path the family can manage, and the "it always plays something" fail-safe: if the stream or the SD card fails, the radio should still play.

Who pays: the adult child (B2C, the Christmas gift that works), the care home (activity budget — 10 units per home is normal), the municipality's dementia-care service (NL/Nordics). Who recommends: OTs, dementia advisers, charity shops. Who uses: the person, who must succeed with one gesture.

Market size (estimates — check them)

  • TAM: 9.07 M people with dementia in the EU27 (12.1 M incl. non-EU Europe); ~40% live at home with family support, ~25% in residential care. If 5% of households and 30% of care homes' residents get a dedicated simple radio/player over 5 years → ~600,000 units ≈ €90 M over 5 years, ~€18 M/year.
  • SAM: DE/NL/UK/Nordics via dementia retailers, charity shops and care-home groups → ~35% → ~€6 M/year.
  • SOM (3 years): 12,000 units ≈ €1.5 M revenue.

Wedge: one knob, wood, the family playlist on an SD card, fail-safe playback, EU DAB/FM-free design (local content first, radio as an SKU), care-home multi-unit setup.

Product & value proposition

  • SKU A — Player: wooden cabinet (oak/birch veneer or solid), 3" full-range driver, 5 W amp, one big weighted knob (on/off + volume), SD-card playlist (family loads MP3s or we pre-load decades playlists), optional single preset internet station via WiFi (SKU A+), mains via a certified external PSU; optional back-panel "carer" buttons under a cover.
  • SKU B — Real radio: the open MW/SW/FM receiver, tuning pre-set and locked by the carer under the back cover, same cabinet and knob. No code, no radio-module cert beyond the receiver itself.
  • Fail-safe: if the playlist file is missing, it plays built-in music; if WiFi drops, it plays the SD card; the knob always works.
  • Pricing: SKU A €149 incl. VAT, SKU A+ (WiFi) €169, SKU B €139; care-home 10-pack €1,190 ex-VAT; retailers 40% off list.

Go-to-market

First 100 customers: 5 care homes' activity coordinators trial 2 units each (the kill question); the dementia charities' online communities and helplines (they recommend products daily); OT and dementia-adviser networks; one family-carer podcast; Christmas gift guides in the 50+ press.

Scale: dementia-care retailers and charity shops (listing fee or margin), care-home groups (procurement), municipal assistive-tech catalogues (NL/Nordics), Amazon with a plain listing, FR/IT with local playlists.

12-month plan

  • Q1: 3D-printed cabinet + Unit AudioPlayer + Unit Encoder prototypes; 5 care homes, 2 weeks each. Gate: residents use it unprompted and coordinators would buy 10 at €139.
  • Q2: Wooden cabinet supplier, own control PCB around a BT/SD player module, decade playlists licensed or public-domain, EMC/LVD, 300 units.
  • Q3: SKU B (real radio), care-home pack, charity-shop listings, 1,000 units.
  • Q4: WiFi SKU with EN 18031 done, FR/IT playlists, 3,000 units.

Team: industrial designer (the product), one electronics/firmware engineer part-time, one dementia-care adviser part-time, founder on sales/sourcing, a cabinet maker/supplier relationship (EU or CN).

Funding to 1,000 units (estimate): NRE €25k (ID, cabinet tooling/jigs €8k, PCB, playlist licensing) + certification €10k + 1,000 × €45 production = €45k → ~€80k; care-home pilots and pre-orders ~€25k → cash need ~€55k. Route: pre-orders, a care-innovation grant (many regions fund dementia-friendly products), and a small angel round only if you add WiFi/streaming.

Top risks

  • Technical — low: a speaker, an amp, a player. The risk is the knob (it must feel like a 1960s radio — weighted, with a click at off) and the playback module's behaviour (no beeps, no voice prompts, auto-resume).
  • Market — volume and retail margin: low volume with distributors eating 40%. Mitigation: direct + care-home packs; keep the cabinet cost down with a flat-pack glued design.
  • Content: pre-loaded music needs licences; mitigation: public-domain recordings for the pre-load, family loads their own, licensed decade packs later.
  • Regulatory: SKU A no-radio = EMC/LVD only (external certified PSU → SELV, EN 62368-1 trivial); SKU A+ WiFi = RED + EN 18031 + CRA; SKU B receiver = RED as a receiver (EN 303 345) — the ideas list said LVD/EMC; check with the lab. No Li-ion (mains only) keeps it simple. No medical claims — "designed for simplicity".
  • Licence: the Jieli standard schematic is a chip-house reference circulating in the community — you buy a module built on it rather than copying it; the OSHWHub radio project needs a licence check.

Firmware/software starting point

  • SKU A needs almost no code: the module plays; the MCU reads the knob and manages power/fail-safe. If you go ESP32 for the WiFi SKU, ESP-ADF (Espressif audio framework, exists) handles SD playback and HTTP streams.
  • Carer setup: a text file on the SD card (station URL, playlist order) and, for the WiFi SKU, a captive-portal web page on the device.

12-week build plan

  • Week 1–2 — validate. Unit AudioPlayer + Unit Encoder + an amp + a 3" driver in a 3D-printed cabinet; 10 units to 5 care homes for 2 weeks. Exit: residents use it unprompted (staff log), coordinators would buy 10 at €139.
  • Week 3–5 — works-like. Module-based control PCB, knob with off-click, fail-safe playback, playlist via SD. Exit: 2 weeks without a "it stopped playing" call.
  • Week 6–8 — looks-like + EVT. Wooden cabinet v1 from a cabinet maker, grille cloth, knob tooling decision, large-print card. Exit: families say "I'd put that in the living room".
  • Week 9–10 — DVT + pre-compliance. EMC pre-scan, PSU and cable checks, drop test in the box, 48 h soak. Exit: pass.
  • Week 11–12 — pilot batch. 50 units: 30 care homes, 20 families. Exit: 30 paid, <5% returns, two care homes order a 10-pack.

Certification checklist (EU)

  • SKU A (no radio): EMC EN 55032/55035, EN 62368-1 (SELV via certified external PSU); self-declaration. Est. €3–5k.
  • SKU A+ (WiFi): RED EN 300 328, EN 301 489-1/-17, EN 18031 (internet-connected), CRA (SBOM, updates). Est. €12–18k.
  • SKU B (receiver): RED as a broadcast receiver (EN 303 345 series) — verify with the lab. Est. €4–8k.
  • RoHS, WEEE, REACH (wood finish, cloth, PVC).
  • No Li-ion (mains only); no GDPR (no accounts; the WiFi SKU must not phone home); no medical claims; not a toy.

Validation toolkit

  • Kill question: in 5 care homes over 2 weeks, do residents use the 3D-printed prototype unprompted, and would coordinators buy 10 at €139? Cheapest experiment: €600 — 10 prototypes from catalogue units, a staff log sheet, five phone calls.
  • 5-question interview (coordinator/family): (1) What does the person listen to today and what stopped working? (2) What happens when a device confuses them — do they give up or get upset? (3) Who would load the music — you, the family, nobody? (4) €139–169 — gift, care budget, or too much? (5) Would a real FM radio or a playlist be the better first product?
  • Landing-page headline: "One knob. Their music. Nothing else to learn." Bullets: Turn it on, it plays — the family playlist or their station · Solid wood, looks like a radio, not an aid · Fail-safe: it always plays something.
  • Pre-order pass: 3 care-home 10-packs or 60 family pre-orders in 6 weeks.

First production checklist

Driver and module samples auditioned; module firmware spec signed (no prompts, auto-play, resume) — get it in writing from the module house; golden sample with a reference SD card; DFM on the cabinet (veneer, glue, grille fit, knob boss); MOQ 300 cabinets with a 2,000 forecast; QC/AQL Level II + 100% play test + knob feel check; packaging drop test (wood corners); sea freight FOB or EU cabinet maker; PSU batch certificates; EU importer + WEEE on the back label; large-print and audio quick-start.
