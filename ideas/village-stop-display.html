Who buys · why now

The buyer in Europe

Landkreise and regional transport associations whose operators already publish GTFS-Realtime (for example VBN and other open-data regions), small municipalities, rural bus operators; B2G procurement.

Rural stops have paper timetables and no information at all when a bus is late; agency displays cost €2,000–5,000 with service contracts; e-paper, Cat-M and solar make a €800 sign possible, and open GTFS data is spreading region by region.

What's new

What you actually have to build

An A4-class e-paper sign in an IP65 housing with a solar panel, a Cat-M or LoRaWAN modem, firmware that always shows the scheduled departures from GTFS and overlays live times only where GTFS-Realtime for that stop actually exists, and says which it is showing.

Certification path

RED (cellular module pre-certified; LoRa variant EN 300 220), EMC, EN 62368-1, RoHS, WEEE, CRA; Battery Regulation for the solar buffer cell; IP65 and vandal-resistance by design, not certification.

Before you cut a PCB

Kill question

Answer it for under €500

Do 2 Landkreise or transport associations whose operators already publish GTFS-Realtime sign a paid 10-stop pilot at €799 per sign within 8 weeks? Pass = 2 signed pilots; a region without GTFS-RT at its stops does not count.
